Stellar astrophysics

Hot subluminous stars in the era of Gaia

I'm a doctoral researcher at the University of Potsdam, working with Prof. Dr. Dr. Stephan Geier. I assembled the first volume-limited sample of hot subluminous stars out to 500 pc — spectroscopically completing every unclassified candidate, which took over 100 nights at ten telescopes and produced more than 5000 spectra.

These stars are stripped remnants, and almost all of them got that way through interaction with a binary companion. That makes them useful well beyond their own classification: they constrain how common envelopes are ejected, they are candidate Type Ia supernova progenitors and LISA verification sources, and they drive the ultraviolet upturn in elliptical galaxies. Most existing samples were assembled from surveys built for unrelated science, so their selection functions were never characterised. A complete sample fixes that — and because it also records what is absent, rare pathways can be constrained rather than written off as bias.

About

The 500 pc sample is 90% complete and holds 397 objects, 305 of which are hot subdwarfs. We measured a mid-plane space density of 6.15 × 10⁻⁷ stars pc⁻³ — roughly an order of magnitude below what binary population synthesis predicts. That discrepancy is the thread I'm still pulling on.

The work spans the full range of methods needed to characterise a stellar population: quantitative spectral analysis, Gaia-based SED fitting, radial velocity and light curve modelling, Galactic kinematics, and population statistics. I've been PI or dedicated PI on observing programmes at 2–8 m facilities, from writing the proposal through to reduction and publication.

Before Potsdam I read physics and mechanical engineering at the University of Western Australia. From February 2027 I'll be a postdoctoral researcher at Vilnius University, Lithuania.
